Combustion

The combustor turns chemical energy into the stagnation temperature the rest of the engine runs on. This module covers how much air a fuel needs (C5), how hot the products get or how much fuel a burner needs (C6), and why the products are never just CO₂ and H₂O at high temperature (C7).

C5Stoichiometry, AF, FA, O/F and φ

Air is modeled on a molar basis as 21% O₂ and 79% N₂, i.e. \(\mathrm{O_2 + 3.76N_2}\). One kmol of "O₂ + 3.76N₂" has mass \(32 + 3.76(28.01) = 137.3\) kg. Atoms are conserved even though molecules are not.

Major products (complete combustion). C → CO₂, H → H₂O, N₂ passes through. Lean (excess O₂): the extra O₂ appears in the products. Rich (too little O₂): the extra H and C appear as H₂ and CO.

Balancing recipe for \(\mathrm{C_xH_y} + a(\mathrm{O_2} + 3.76\mathrm{N_2})\): basis of 1 kmol fuel; balance C, then H, then O; nitrogen is \(3.76a\). Stoichiometric \(a_{st} = x + y/4\). For methane, \(\mathrm{CH_4 + 2(O_2 + 3.76N_2) \to CO_2 + 2H_2O + 7.52N_2}\). For decane at φ = 0.5:

$$\mathrm{C_{10}H_{22} + 31(O_2 + 3.76N_2) \to 10CO_2 + 11H_2O + 116.56N_2 + 15.5O_2}.$$

Mixture measures.

$$AF = \frac{m_{air}}{m_{fuel}},\qquad FA = f = \frac{\dot m_f}{\dot m_0} = \frac1{AF},\qquad O/F = \frac{m_{ox}}{m_{fuel}},\qquad \phi = \frac{FA_{actual}}{FA_{st}} = \frac{AF_{st}}{AF_{actual}}.$$

\(\phi < 1\) is lean and \(\phi > 1\) is rich. % theoretical air = \(100a/a_{st} = 100/\phi\). Gas-turbine combustors run lean overall (φ ≈ 0.4, to keep \(T_{t4}\) survivable); rockets run rich (φ ≈ 1.3: lighter exhaust raises Isp). European texts sometimes use the inverse (λ = 1/φ). Example: LOX/methane stoichiometric \(O/F = 2(32)/16 = 4\); the BE-4 at O/F ≈ 3 runs at φ ≈ 4/3.

C6Energy balance, heating values and flame temperature

Reference state. Chemical energy is counted with enthalpies of formation at 298 K and 1 atm (\(\bar h_f^\circ\), Table A-25), with \(\bar h_f^\circ = 0\) for stable elements (O₂, N₂, H₂, C). Each species then carries

$$\bar h_i(T) = \bar h_{f,i}^\circ + [\bar h_i(T) - \bar h_i(298\,\text{K})],$$

the formation enthalpy plus the sensible part from Table A-23. For a steady combustor with negligible KE and PE:

$$\dot Q - \dot W + \sum_R\dot N_i\bar h_i - \sum_P\dot N_i\bar h_i = 0.$$

Enthalpy of reaction and heating value. \(\Delta H_{rxn}^\circ = \sum_P n_i\bar h_{f,i}^\circ - \sum_R n_j\bar h_{f,j}^\circ\), negative for exothermic reactions. The heating value is \(-\Delta H_{rxn}^\circ\) per unit mass of fuel for stoichiometric combustion: LHV with H₂O as gas (what engines use; it is the \(Q_R\) of cycle analysis), HHV with H₂O as liquid. For decane (gas, \(\bar h_f^\circ \approx -249{,}500\) kJ/kmol): \(\Delta H = 10(-393{,}520) + 11(-241{,}820) - (-249{,}500) = -6.35\times10^6\) kJ/kmol, so LHV ≈ 44,600 kJ/kg.

Adiabatic flame temperature. With \(\dot Q = \dot W = 0\), all the chemical energy becomes sensible energy, \(H_R(T_R) = H_P(T_{ad})\). Iterate: guess \(T\) (start by pretending all the products are N₂), evaluate \(H_P\) from the tables, bracket and interpolate linearly. \(T_{ad}\) depends on the fuel and oxidizer, φ (it peaks near φ ≈ 1–1.05), the reactant temperature, and the product model. Major-products estimates are too high at high T because dissociation absorbs energy (C7).

Burner model for cycle analysis. With a constant \(c_p\) and burner efficiency \(\eta_b\), the energy balance per unit air mass flow is

$$c_pT_{t3} + \eta_bfQ_R = (1 + f)c_pT_{t4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f = \frac{c_p(T_{t4} - T_{t3})}{\eta_bQ_R - c_pT_{t4}}.$$

This links C6 to the cycles in Module 3: the burner exit temperature fixes \(f\), and \(f/f_{st}\) is the overall φ.

C7Chemical equilibrium

For a rich LOX/methane mixture with products CO₂, CO, H₂O, H₂, the atom balance gives 3 equations for 4 unknowns. The extra equation comes from the second law: at fixed \(T\) and \(p\), the products settle where the Gibbs energy \(G = H - TS\) is a minimum (like a ball rolling to the bottom of a bowl). CEA and STANJAN minimize \(G\) directly; by hand we use equilibrium constants.

For an ideal-gas reaction \(\nu_AA + \nu_BB \rightleftharpoons \nu_CC + \nu_DD\) (equation sheet form):

$$K_p(T) = \frac{x_C^{\nu_C}x_D^{\nu_D}}{x_A^{\nu_A}x_B^{\nu_B}}\left(\frac{p}{p_0}\right)^{\Delta n} = \frac{n_C^{\nu_C}n_D^{\nu_D}}{n_A^{\nu_A}n_B^{\nu_B}}\left(\frac{p/p_0}{n_{tot}}\right)^{\Delta n},\qquad \Delta n = \sum_P\nu - \sum_R\nu.$$

Rules. \(K_p\) depends on \(T\) only. Reverse a reaction → \(1/K\). Multiply by \(n\) → \(K^n\). Add reactions → multiply their \(K\)s (add the logs). Table A-27 lists \(\log_{10}K\).

Trends (Le Chatelier). Raising \(T\) raises \(K\) for dissociation reactions, so more CO, H₂, O, H and OH appear at high temperature. Raising \(p\) favors the side with fewer moles when \(\Delta n \ne 0\): more pressure, less dissociation. The water–gas shift \(\mathrm{CO_2 + H_2 \rightleftharpoons CO + H_2O}\) has \(\Delta n = 0\), so pressure does not move it; higher T shifts it toward CO.

Recipe for dissociation of 1 kmol CO₂ at \(T\), \(p\): products \((1 - \alpha)\mathrm{CO_2} + \alpha\mathrm{CO} + \tfrac\alpha2\mathrm{O_2}\), \(n = 1 + \alpha/2\), \(\Delta n = \tfrac12\); solve \(K = \dfrac{\alpha(\alpha/2)^{1/2}}{1 - \alpha}\left(\dfrac{p}{1 + \alpha/2}\right)^{1/2}\) for \(\alpha\) by iteration.
