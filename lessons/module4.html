Rocket Propulsion

A rocket carries its own oxidizer, so it works in space, and its whole performance comes from a hot chamber feeding a nozzle. This module covers the rocket equation and how to size an engine from chamber conditions and nozzle geometry (C10), solid motor ballistics (C11), and how to judge a design choice against a criterion (J3).

In general \(I_{sp,liquid} > I_{sp,hybrid} > I_{sp,solid}\) while \(\lambda_{solid} > \lambda_{hybrid} > \lambda_{liquid}\). The two figures of merit are specific impulse and propellant mass fraction.

Lecture 15The rocket equation and mass fractions

Masses: payload \(m_{pl}\), propellant \(m_p\), inert \(m_i\). Initial \(m_0 = m_{pl} + m_p + m_i\), final \(m_f = m_{pl} + m_i\).

$$\Delta v = g_0I_{sp}\ln\frac{m_0}{m_f} - \int_0^{t_b}g\cos\theta\,dt - \text{drag and steering losses},\qquad MR = \frac{m_0}{m_f},\qquad \lambda = \frac{m_p}{m_p + m_i}.$$

Solving for the propellant needed (equation sheet): \(m_p = m_{pl}\dfrac{MR - 1}{MR - (MR - 1)/\lambda}\) and \(m_i = m_p(1 - \lambda)/\lambda\). If the denominator is zero or negative, no single stage can do it. Reaching LEO takes about 9.3–10 km/s including about 1.5–2 km/s of gravity and drag losses, so one stage is impractical. Trajectories pitch over early so most acceleration happens high up and nearly horizontal, keeping g·t losses low.

C10Chamber, c*, C_F and Isp

Treat the chamber as the stagnation state (\(p_c = p_t\), \(T_c = T_t\)), assume steady, adiabatic, frozen, isentropic flow of an ideal gas with constant \(\gamma\) and \(\mathcal M\), and a choked throat. Then the mass flow, thrust and specific impulse are

$$\dot m = \frac{p_cA_t}{c^*},\qquad F = \dot mu_e + (p_e - p_a)A_e = C_Fp_cA_t,\qquad I_{sp} = \frac{F}{\dot mg_0} = \frac{C_Fc^*}{g_0}.$$

The characteristic velocity depends only on the combustion (hot, light exhaust is best):

$$c^* = \sqrt{\frac{\bar RT_c}{\mathcal M\gamma}}\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{-\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}.$$

The thrust coefficient depends only on the nozzle (\(\gamma\), \(\varepsilon = A_e/A_t\), \(p_e/p_c\), \(p_a/p_c\)):

$$C_F = \sqrt{\frac{2\gamma^2}{\gamma-1}\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}}\left[1 - \left(\frac{p_e}{p_c}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\right]} + \left(\frac{p_e}{p_c} - \frac{p_a}{p_c}\right)\varepsilon,\qquad C_F = C_{F,vac} - \frac{p_a}{p_c}\varepsilon.$$

\(p_e/p_c\) comes from \(\varepsilon\) through the isentropic area–Mach relation (supersonic branch, with the exhaust \(\gamma\)). Altitude correction: \(I_{sp} = I_{sp,vac} - p_aA_e/(\dot mg_0)\). For a given altitude, \(C_F\) is maximized when \(p_e = p_a\) (perfect expansion). In vacuum a bigger \(\varepsilon\) always helps, but with diminishing returns and growing nozzle mass.

Sizing recipe: combustion gives \(T_c\), \(\mathcal M\), \(\gamma\) → \(c^*\); choose \(p_c\) and \(\varepsilon\) → \(p_e/p_c\) → \(C_F\) at the design altitude; thrust requirement → \(A_t = F/(C_Fp_c)\), \(A_e = \varepsilon A_t\), \(\dot m = p_cA_t/c^*\). Real engines get about 92–98% of the ideal \(c^*\) and \(C_F\) (incomplete combustion, heat loss, divergence and boundary-layer losses).

Heister's first law and Canino's counter

Isp only measures propellant efficiency. Vehicle performance also depends on density (tank mass), \(\lambda\), thrust-to-weight and operations, which is why dense, low-Isp first stages (kerosene, solids) and light, high-Isp upper stages (hydrogen) coexist.

C11Solid rocket motors

Parts: case, grain (propellant) with a port, liner/insulation, igniter (pyrogen = small motor; pyrotechnic = charge), nozzle. Propellants: double-base (homogeneous NC/NG) or composite (AP oxidizer ~60–70%, Al fuel ~18%, HTPB or PBAN binder ~12%). Shuttle SRB exhaust was about 24% H₂, 20% CO, 18% H₂O, 17% HCl, 9% N₂ and 6% Al₂O₃ by mole.

Burn rate. The surface regresses normal to itself at rate \(r_b\), modeled by St. Robert's (Vieille's) law \(r_b = ap_c^n\), with \(n \approx 0.3\)–0.5 typical. Watch the units of \(a\): they carry \(p^{-n}\).

Steady lumped-parameter model. Treat the whole chamber as one control volume at uniform pressure (low port Mach number, quasi-steady, frozen, constant \(T_t\)). Mass generated = mass through the choked throat:

$$\rho_pA_br_b = \frac{p_cA_t}{c^*}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_c = \left(\frac{a\rho_pA_bc^*}{A_t}\right)^{\frac1{1-n}},\qquad F = C_Fp_cA_t.$$

(The equation sheet writes \(g_c\) in the denominator for US units.) Since \(p_c \propto (A_b/A_t)^{1/(1-n)}\), the chamber pressure follows the burning area. With \(n < 1\) the operating point is stable: a pressure bump raises the outflow (∝ p) faster than the generation (∝ pⁿ), so it decays. With \(n \ge 1\) it runs away. Small \(n\) also means low sensitivity to cracks and temperature.

Grain design. Progressive (\(A_b\), \(p_c\), F rise: an internal-burning tube), regressive (fall: a rod burning outward, good for boosters needing maximum thrust at t = 0), neutral (nearly constant: star, wagon-wheel, rod-and-tube). Burn time ≈ web thickness / average burn rate. Prediction runs the model forward from geometry to \(p_c(t)\); reconstruction runs it backward from a measured \(p_c(t)\) to \(A_b(t)\).

J3Evaluating design choices

J3 gives you a performance criterion and a design choice to evaluate. Use the same structure as J2: state the criterion → identify the relation that connects the choice to it → decide → name the cost. The relations you need are almost always one of these:

  • \(\Delta v = g_0I_{sp}\ln MR\): Isp versus mass fraction, for payload or Δv criteria.
  • \(I_{sp} = C_Fc^*/g_0\), \(c^* \propto \sqrt{T_c/\mathcal M}\): propellant choice, mixture ratio (rich lowers 𝓜).
  • \(C_F(\varepsilon, p_a/p_c)\): nozzle area ratio for an altitude range; separation risk at sea level.
  • \(p_c \propto (A_b/A_t)^{1/(1-n)}\): grain shape for a thrust profile; burn-rate exponent for stability.
  • Operational criteria: storability, response time, throttling, reuse, testability, cost and safety.
