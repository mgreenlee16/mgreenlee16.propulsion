Heat Transfer

Combustors, turbine blades and rocket throats sit in gas far hotter than any metal can survive. Keeping them alive is a heat-transfer problem. This module covers the three modes, resistance networks and 1-D steady conduction, choosing and applying a convective correlation (C12), and fins (C13).

Lecture 18Modes and the energy balance

Thermal energy \(U\) (J), temperature \(T\) (its driving potential), heat \(Q\) (J), heat rate \(q\) (W), heat flux \(q''\) (W/m²). The energy balance is "in − out + generated = stored": \(\dot E_{in} - \dot E_{out} + \dot E_g = \dot E_{st}\).

ModeRate equationNeeds
Conduction (Fourier)\(q = -kA\nabla T\); 1-D: \(q_x = -kA\,dT/dx\)a medium, solid or stationary fluid
Convection (Newton)\(q = hA(T_s - T_\infty)\)a moving fluid
Radiation\(q = \varepsilon\sigma A(T_s^4 - T_{sur}^4)\), \(\sigma = 5.67\times10^{-8}\) W/m²·K⁴nothing (photons)

Radiation can be linearized: \(q = h_rA(T_s - T_{sur})\) with \(h_r = \varepsilon\sigma(T_s + T_{sur})(T_s^2 + T_{sur}^2)\). Always use kelvin for radiation.

Lecture 191-D steady conduction and thermal resistance

The heat equation (equation sheet): \(\nabla\cdot(k\nabla T) + \dot q = \rho c_p\,\partial T/\partial t\). For steady 1-D conduction with no generation and constant \(k\), the plane-wall temperature is linear and the cylinder's is logarithmic. Each piece acts like an electrical resistor, \(R = \Delta T/q\):

$$R_{wall} = \frac{L}{kA},\qquad R_{cyl} = \frac{\ln(r_2/r_1)}{2\pi kL},\qquad R_{conv} = \frac{1}{hA},\qquad R_{rad} = \frac{1}{h_rA}.$$

Series resistances add; parallel ones add as reciprocals. Then \(q = (T_{\infty,1} - T_{\infty,2})/R_{tot}\), and you walk through the network to get each interface temperature. The biggest resistance takes the biggest temperature drop, which is how a thin ceramic thermal-barrier coating protects a turbine blade. Contact resistances \(R''_{t,c}\) add in series at joints. For a cylinder, adding insulation can increase heat loss until \(r > r_{cr} = k/h\).

With uniform generation \(\dot q\) in a plane wall of half-thickness \(L\) with both surfaces at \(T_s\): \(T(x) = \frac{\dot qL^2}{2k}\left(1 - \frac{x^2}{L^2}\right) + T_s\).

C12Convective correlations

Convection coefficients come from dimensionless correlations \(Nu = f(Re, Pr)\), with \(Re_\ell = \rho V\ell/\mu\), \(Nu_\ell = h\ell/k_f\) and \(Pr = c_p\mu/k = \nu/\alpha\). C12 is about selecting the right one. Ask in order: internal or external flow? Laminar or turbulent (compute Re)? Heating or cooling, and is the surface at constant \(T_s\) or constant \(q''_s\)? Which temperature do I evaluate properties at (film \(T_f = (T_s + T_\infty)/2\) for external flow, mean bulk temperature for internal)? Is the flow high speed (use the recovery temperature)? Check the correlation's validity range.

High-speed flow. Friction heating in the boundary layer means the wall heats toward the adiabatic wall (recovery) temperature \(T_{aw} = T_g\left(1 + r\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2\right)\), with \(r = Pr^{1/3}\) (turbulent; \(Pr^{1/2}\) laminar). Use \(q'' = h(T_{aw} - T_w)\). Heat flux in a rocket nozzle peaks near the throat, where \(\rho V\) is largest.

Internal flow energy balance. For constant surface temperature, the mean temperature approaches \(T_s\) exponentially (equation sheet):

$$\frac{\Delta T_o}{\Delta T_i} = \frac{T_s - T_{m,o}}{T_s - T_{m,i}} = \exp\left(-\frac{\bar hPL}{\dot mc_p}\right),\qquad q = \bar hA_s\Delta T_{lm},\quad \Delta T_{lm} = \frac{\Delta T_o - \Delta T_i}{\ln(\Delta T_o/\Delta T_i)}.$$

For a fixed outside fluid temperature \(T_\infty\) instead of \(T_s\), replace \(\bar hPL\) with \(\bar UA_s = 1/R_{tot}\). This is how regenerative cooling channels and heat exchangers are sized.

C13Fins and fin arrays

A fin adds surface area for convection. An energy balance on a slice of a fin with constant cross-section \(A_c\) and perimeter \(P\) gives \(d^2\theta/dx^2 - m^2\theta = 0\) with \(\theta = T - T_\infty\) and \(m^2 = hP/(kA_c)\). With \(M = \sqrt{hPkA_c}\,\theta_b\):

Tip conditionFin heat rate \(q_f\)
Convective tip\(M\dfrac{\sinh mL + (h/mk)\cosh mL}{\cosh mL + (h/mk)\sinh mL}\)
Adiabatic tip\(M\tanh mL\) (use the corrected length \(L_c = L + D/4\) for a pin or \(L + t/2\) for a plate to approximate a convective tip)
Fixed tip temperature\(M\dfrac{\cosh mL - \theta_L/\theta_b}{\sinh mL}\)
Infinite fin (\(mL > 2.65\))\(M\)

Fin efficiency \(\eta_f = q_f/(hA_f\theta_b)\) compares the fin with an ideal one that is all at \(T_b\); for an adiabatic tip, \(\eta_f = \tanh(mL)/(mL)\). Effectiveness \(\varepsilon_f = q_f/(hA_{c,b}\theta_b)\) compares it with no fin; it should be at least 2. Fins pay off most with high \(k\), thin sections (large \(P/A_c\)) and low \(h\) (gases, not liquids). Arrays: \(A_t = NA_f + A_b\), \(\eta_o = 1 - \frac{NA_f}{A_t}(1 - \eta_f)\), \(q_t = \eta_ohA_t\theta_b\).
